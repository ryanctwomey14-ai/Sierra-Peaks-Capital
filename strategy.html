Sierra Peaks / Strategy

Investment criteria.

Sierra Peaks acquires underperforming workforce apartment communities in selected growth markets, addresses the operational and physical deficiencies of prior ownership, and finances each asset so that a period of weak performance does not become a forced sale.

Our acquisition criteria.

Acquisition criteria are only useful if they are specific enough to disqualify. A property must satisfy all seven of the following before it proceeds to full underwriting.

  • 01

    150 to 400 units

    Large enough to carry a full-time on-site team and absorb a vacancy without drama. Small enough that the institutional buyers with a lower cost of capital are not bidding against us.

  • 02

    Built 1985 to 2010

    Old enough to buy below replacement cost and improve. New enough that the plumbing stack, the roof and the electrical panel are not going to become the entire business plan.

  • 03

    Class B, workforce rents

    We target rents between 70% and 100% of the submarket median. Households at that level cannot afford to buy and cannot afford to trade down. That is the most defensible tenant base in a recession.

  • 04

    A specific, measurable inefficiency

    Below-market rents under an absentee owner, an unrenovated unit mix, an unbilled utility, a mismanaged expense line. If we cannot name the inefficiency in one sentence, there is no business plan — only a bet on the market.

  • 05

    Constrained supply pipeline

    Fewer than 5% of existing submarket inventory under construction or permitted within a three-mile radius. This constraint was added after Alder Point, and it has since disqualified eleven deals we otherwise liked.

  • 06

    Growth markets with real employers

    Metro population growth above the national average, job growth across at least three unrelated sectors, and a median household income that supports our rents at under 30% of gross income.

  • 07

    Financeable with fixed-rate agency debt

    If the asset cannot support a five-year-minimum fixed-rate Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac loan at 60–70% loan-to-value with a 1.30× debt service coverage ratio on trailing income, we pass. This single rule is why we still own everything we have ever bought.

Target markets.

We look for emerging markets where growth is still ahead of pricing — locations where the fundamentals have turned but the capital markets have not yet fully repriced them. Our criteria are applied consistently, regardless of geography.

Economic development

We prioritise markets with committed infrastructure investment and a diversifying employer base — where development is already underway and funded rather than announced.

Constrained supply

We look for submarkets where new deliveries are limited by land availability, construction cost or entitlement difficulty, so incoming demand is absorbed by existing stock.

Demand depth

We target locations with sustained household formation and net in-migration, where rental demand is broad-based rather than dependent on a single employer or sector.

Population & job growth

We look for population and employment growth in the range of 1.5% to 2% year over year, sustained across multiple cycles rather than driven by a single expansion.

Focused. Disciplined. Repeatable.

At Sierra Peaks Capital, every investment begins with a clear, well-defined plan. From market selection to execution and exit, each step is intentional and aligned with a structured strategy designed to create value and drive performance.

We focus on identifying the right opportunities, executing with precision, and positioning each asset for a successful outcome.

01

Sourcing & acquisition

Every investment begins with a clear, well-defined plan. We identify the right opportunity before we ever talk about price.

Market selection

We target emerging and high-growth markets by analysing a wide range of economic indicators, including population trends, job growth and local demand drivers. We also evaluate where each market sits within its cycle — and where it is likely to be at the time of exit — so we invest with both entry and exit in mind.

Rigorous underwriting

Every opportunity is evaluated through a detailed underwriting process that stress-tests assumptions and confirms alignment with our investment criteria. We build clear, well-structured business plans supported by realistic projections and defined execution strategies.

Selective acquisitions

We only pursue properties that present clear upside potential and align with our strategy. Each acquisition is made with intention, ensuring the asset has a defined path to increased performance and long-term value creation.

02

Thorough due diligence

Before any acquisition is finalised we validate every aspect of the investment and confirm the business plan. This is what lets us move forward with clarity, confidence and a fully aligned execution plan.

Financial review

We verify income, expenses, leases and historical performance to ensure accuracy and to identify opportunities for improvement.

Physical inspections

We evaluate the condition of the property — units, major systems and deferred maintenance — to fully understand the capital requirements before we commit.

Operational analysis

We assess current property management, the tenant profile and operational efficiencies to identify opportunities for immediate impact after acquisition.

03

Driving value through execution

Our strategy focuses on increasing both income and asset value through intentional improvements. Each property is managed with a clear plan to elevate performance and unlock its full potential.

Renovations & upgrades

We improve unit interiors and property conditions to support higher rents, improve tenant quality and enhance the overall asset.

Operational improvements

We refine property management, leasing strategy and expense control to increase efficiency and overall performance.

Revenue optimisation

We implement strategies to grow income through better pricing, improved amenities and additional revenue opportunities.

04

Active asset management

Execution continues throughout the hold period with a hands-on, performance-focused approach. We actively manage each asset so it stays aligned with the business plan.

Performance tracking

We monitor key metrics such as occupancy, rent growth and expenses to confirm the property is progressing as planned.

Cost efficiency

We maintain a strong focus on controlling expenses while preserving property quality and tenant satisfaction.

Cash flow optimisation

We work to increase and stabilise income over time, strengthening both cash flow and overall asset value.

05

Strategic exit planning

Each investment is structured with a clear exit strategy from the beginning, typically targeting a three to five year hold depending on market conditions. We remain flexible and evaluate market timing to determine the optimal point to exit.

Market timing

We assess economic trends, demand and capital markets to identify the strongest window to sell.

Value realisation

We exit once the business plan has been fully executed and the asset has reached its highest potential.

Investor returns

Our goal is to deliver strong outcomes through both consistent cash flow during the hold period and maximum value at exit.

Acquire the right asset. Execute with precision. Exit at the optimal time.

That is the whole strategy. This disciplined approach is what drives performance and creates meaningful results for our investors.

Risk controls.

Each of these constraints disqualifies opportunities we would otherwise pursue.

See how they have performed
  • ·

    Fixed-rate debt only

    Minimum five-year term, agency or life-company. No floating-rate bridge debt, no rate caps expiring inside the hold period. This is the single most common cause of sponsor failure and it is entirely avoidable.

  • ·

    Underwrite trailing, not projected

    Income is modelled on the trailing three months of actual collections — not the rent roll, not the seller’s pro forma, and never on assumed rent growth above 3% annually.

  • ·

    Twelve months of debt service in reserve

    Funded at closing, from the raise, before any distribution is made. It is the difference between a difficult year and a capital call.

  • ·

    Exit at a wider cap rate than entry

    We assume we sell into a worse market than we bought in — typically 50 to 75 basis points wider. If the deal only works at a flat exit cap, it does not work.
