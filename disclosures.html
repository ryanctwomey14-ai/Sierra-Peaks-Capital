Disclosures and risk factors.

The complete legal position on what this website is, what it is not, and the risks of investing in a private real estate offering.

This page is a template, not legal advice. It sets out the disclosures a Rule 506(c) sponsor site typically carries. Have your securities counsel review and adapt every paragraph before publication. Desert Peaks Capital and its advisers are responsible for the final content.

No offer of securities

Nothing on this website constitutes an offer to sell, or the solicitation of an offer to buy, any security, nor shall there be any sale of securities in any jurisdiction in which such an offer, solicitation or sale would be unlawful. Any offer will be made solely through a confidential private placement memorandum, subscription agreement and limited liability company operating agreement (the “Offering Documents”) delivered to verified accredited investors. In the event of any inconsistency between this website and the Offering Documents, the Offering Documents govern.

Regulation D, Rule 506(c)

Interests are offered pursuant to Rule 506(c) of Regulation D under the Securities Act of 1933, as amended. Participation is limited to accredited investors as defined in Rule 501(a), and the issuer will take reasonable steps to verify each investor’s accredited status through an independent third party. Self-certification is not sufficient. Interests have not been registered under the Securities Act or any state securities laws and may not be resold absent registration or an applicable exemption.

Principal risk factors

An investment in a private real estate offering is speculative and involves a high degree of risk, including the risk of losing your entire investment. The risks below are a summary and are not exhaustive; the Offering Documents contain the complete risk factors and should be read in full.

  • Loss of capital. You may lose some or all of your invested capital. No return of capital is guaranteed.
  • Illiquidity. There is no public market for these interests and none is expected to develop. Transfers are restricted and generally require sponsor consent. You should be prepared to hold for the entire term and beyond.
  • Leverage. Properties are financed with mortgage debt, which magnifies both gains and losses. A decline in property value can eliminate equity entirely.
  • No guaranteed distributions. Distributions are made from available cash flow at the discretion of the manager and may be reduced, suspended or discontinued at any time.
  • Real estate market risk. Values and rents are affected by interest rates, employment, local supply, insurance and tax costs, regulation including rent control, and general economic conditions, none of which the manager controls.
  • Operator and key-person risk. Results depend substantially on the manager’s judgement and on the continued involvement of its principals.
  • Concentration. Offerings may hold a single property or a small number of properties in a limited number of markets, without the diversification of a broad fund.
  • Conflicts of interest. The manager receives fees regardless of investor returns, may sponsor competing offerings, and allocates time across multiple assets. Conflicts are described in the Offering Documents.
  • Tax risk. Tax treatment depends on your circumstances and on legislation that may change, potentially with retroactive effect. Depreciation is subject to recapture on disposition. K-1 reporting may require state filings and extensions.
  • Capital calls and dilution. Additional capital may be required. Declining to participate may result in dilution of your interest.
  • Insurance and casualty risk. Some losses are uninsurable or insurable only at prohibitive cost. Insurance premiums in several of our markets have risen sharply.
