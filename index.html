The investors we work with.

Our limited partners generally come to us from one of six situations.

01

Physicians and medical professionals

Specialists, surgeons and dentists in the top marginal bracket who have already maximised their qualified retirement accounts. The requirement is usually depreciation that can be applied against passive income, and distributions that do not depend on additional clinical hours.

02

Executives with concentrated equity

Senior operators whose vested equity has become a substantial share of net worth. The objective is diversification into an asset valued on net operating income rather than public market sentiment, with more favourable tax treatment than an outright sale of the position.

03

Business owners following an exit

A liquidity event converts an operating business into cash and a significant tax liability. The typical objectives are diversification away from a single concentrated outcome and the generation of income without an operating role.

04

Existing direct property owners

Investors who already hold one to four rental units and want continued real estate exposure without direct management responsibility. Passive ownership of a professionally managed community provides the same asset class exposure without the operating burden.

05

1031 exchange investors

Investors working within the 45-day identification and 180-day closing windows. These matters are time-sensitive; we recommend calling directly rather than emailing so we can assess feasibility quickly.

06

Self-directed retirement accounts

Self-directed IRAs and solo 401(k)s may hold private real estate, but the two vehicles are treated differently under leverage. We will review UBIT exposure and custodian mechanics with you, including circumstances in which retirement capital is not the appropriate source of funds.

Three sources of return.

A well-acquired apartment community generates return through three independent mechanisms, each with a different tax treatment.

Current income

Rental income is collected monthly and is not correlated to public market pricing. After debt service and reserve funding, the remainder is distributed to limited partners quarterly, beginning in the first full quarter following stabilisation.

Tax-advantaged distributions

Real property is depreciated for tax purposes while the asset may appreciate in value. A cost segregation study accelerates a portion of that depreciation into the early years of the hold, and the resulting losses are passed through to limited partners on a Schedule K-1.

Equity appreciation

Rental income amortises the mortgage over the hold period. We increase asset value by increasing net operating income through renovation, expense control and improved management, then refinance or sell. Gains are generally treated as long-term capital gains.

Why workforce housing.

Housing demand is driven by household formation rather than by business conditions, which distinguishes workforce housing from other commercial real estate. We acquire in markets receiving sustained net domestic migration.

Net domestic migration · where Americans are movingInto Idaho, Utah, Arizona, Texas, the Carolinas and Florida

  • 4.5M Homes short

    The estimated shortfall between U.S. household formation and housing supply — a structural deficit that took two decades to build.

  • 38% Cost gap to buy

    The premium of an average new mortgage payment over average market rent, keeping high-earning renters renting for longer.

  • 12 mo Lease duration

    Rents reset annually. Unlike a ten-year office lease, an apartment portfolio re-prices to inflation every single year.

  • 60+ yr Agency debt market

    Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac provide non-recourse, long-term, fixed-rate financing to apartments. No other asset class has that backstop.
