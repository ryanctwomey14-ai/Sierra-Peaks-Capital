Eligibility & getting started

You qualify if you earned over $200,000 individually or $300,000 jointly in each of the last two years and reasonably expect the same this year, or if your net worth excluding your primary residence exceeds $1 million. Holding an active Series 7, 65 or 82 licence also qualifies you, as does being a knowledgeable employee of certain private funds.

Entities qualify too: trusts and LLCs with over $5 million in assets, or entities where every equity owner is individually accredited.

Because this is a Rule 506(c) offering, the law requires reasonable steps to verify accreditation — self-certification is not enough. We use an independent third-party verification service.

You can satisfy it with a signed letter from your CPA, attorney, registered investment adviser or broker-dealer confirming your status. Alternatively you can provide tax returns or asset statements directly to the verification service. Sierra Peaks receives only a pass or fail result; we never see the underlying financial documents.

Money, fees and returns

We underwrite to a 1.8× to 2.2× equity multiple over a three to seven year hold, with a 7–10% annual cash-on-cash distribution beginning after stabilisation. That corresponds to a 15–20% target net internal rate of return.

These are targets derived from our underwriting assumptions, not promises. Assumptions can be wrong. Our realised results across four full-cycle assets have averaged 1.9×, with a range from 1.41× to 2.14× — that range is the honest picture.

Quarterly, by ACH, within 30 days of each quarter end. On a stabilised acquisition, distributions typically begin in the first full quarter after closing. On a heavier value-add project, they may be deferred six to twelve months while capital goes into the building — the offering documents state the expectation for each specific deal before you commit.

A 1.5% acquisition fee on purchase price at closing, a 1.5% annual asset management fee on collected revenue, and a 25% performance promote on profits above an 8% compounded preferred return after all capital has been returned.

There is no disposition fee, no financing fee, no construction management fee and no investor servicing fee. Every fee is set out in full in the private placement memorandum.

Tax

In the early years, typically a taxable loss — often larger than the cash you received — because accelerated depreciation from the cost segregation study exceeds the property’s taxable income. In later years, as depreciation declines and income rises, it turns positive. In the year of sale you will have capital gain plus depreciation recapture.

Your K-1 is issued annually for your tax filing.

Usually not. Passive losses generally offset passive income only, and they carry forward indefinitely until you have passive income or you sell the asset — at which point suspended losses are released.

The exception is real estate professional status, which requires more than 750 hours and more than half your working time in real property trades. If your spouse qualifies, a joint return may allow those losses against ordinary income. This is a decision for your CPA, not for us.

The depreciation you deducted along the way is recaptured on sale, taxed at up to 25% rather than the long-term capital gains rate. It converts a permanent saving into a deferral — but a deferral across three to seven years, at your marginal rate, invested in the meantime, is worth a great deal. A 1031 exchange or a step-up in basis at death can extend the deferral further.

Risk, structure and control

Values decline and, if a sale were forced at that moment, so would returns. What protects you is that we do not have to sell: fixed-rate debt with a minimum five-year term means no lender can force a refinance into a bad market, and twelve months of reserves means a soft period does not become a liquidity event. Our response is generally to extend the hold and keep collecting rent.

The operating agreement provides for continuity: the surviving partner has authority to operate, and we maintain key-person life insurance sufficient to fund a transition or an orderly sale. Property management, lending and accounting relationships are held at the firm level, not personally.

There is no secondary market and no redemption right. Transfers require sponsor consent and are permitted only in limited circumstances such as estate planning. Treat this capital as committed for the full hold period — if that is a problem, this is the wrong investment for you.

Limited partners are passive by design and by law — that passivity is part of what preserves your limited liability. You do not vote on renovations, leasing or budgets. Major decisions such as a sale, a refinance or a change of sponsor are governed by the operating agreement, which sets out where limited partner consent is required.
