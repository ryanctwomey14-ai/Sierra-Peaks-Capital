Case study: Alder Point.

Alder Point returned 1.41× against a 1.85× target. Investors received a positive return. The full account is set out below.

  • 01

    What we saw

    A 148-unit 1996-vintage community in north Spokane at $118,000 per unit — roughly 40% below replacement cost. Rents sat 14% under submarket, the owner had not renovated a unit since 2014, and expenses ran 8% above comparable properties. The operational thesis was sound and, in the event, it worked.

  • 02

    What we missed

    We modelled the submarket supply pipeline from permits already issued. We did not model land under contract or projects in pre-application. Within eighteen months of closing, 1,900 competing units delivered into a submarket with roughly 11,000 total units. Concessions became universal and our rent premiums evaporated on delivery.

  • 03

    What we did

    We stopped the renovation programme at unit 61 of a planned 148 rather than spending capital into a market that would not pay for it. We shifted to retention — renewal incentives, longer lease terms — and protected occupancy at 93% while competitors bought traffic with two months free. We held eight months past our target to let the supply wave absorb.

  • 04

    What it cost, and what changed

    Investors received 1.41× over 3.8 years, a 10.2% net IRR. A positive result, and a clear miss against target. Our fixed-rate agency debt is the reason it was a disappointment rather than a loss — a floating-rate bridge loan in that period would have wiped out the equity. Criterion 05 of our buy box exists because of this deal, and it has since disqualified eleven properties we would otherwise have pursued.

Why this is published

A track record is only informative if it includes the investments that underperformed, and how the sponsor responded to them.

Most sponsors with a track record have a comparable investment. The relevant questions are whether it is disclosed before you invest, what was changed as a result, and whether the capital structure protected investor capital in the interim.
